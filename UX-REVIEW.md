## Attention and trust mechanisms applied

| Reference observation | Design interpretation | Application |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Large name on a restrained black masthead | Strong contrast and few competing elements establish visual hierarchy | Centered name, short specialty line and one primary project action |
| Personal introduction and an easy-to-scan journey | A human voice and chronological anchors help orient the reader | Plain first-person introduction and source-supported academic/work progression |
| Recognizable organizations and specific responsibilities | Concrete context can support credibility; brand recognition alone does not prove competence | Actual organization names and concise contributions; no borrowed logos or prestige claims |
| Large engineering imagery next to explanation | Relevant evidence can help readers understand what was done | Project prototypes and fabrication photos next to the owner's contribution |
| More detail on separate project pages | A short overview lets readers choose how deeply to explore | 12 full case-study pages, direct URLs, category filters and next/previous navigation |
| Technical choices explained through constraints | Reasoning provides stronger evidence than a list of tools | Existing constraints and methods retained; missing decision rationales requested rather than invented |

General UX sources consulted:
- https://www.nngroup.com/articles/visual-hierarchy-ux-definition/
- https://www.nngroup.com/articles/trustworthy-design/

No specific visitor behavior, conversion rate or psychological effect is promised. Personal warmth should come from the owner's actual interests and stories, not borrowed humor.
